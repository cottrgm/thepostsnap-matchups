The ratings

Four independent rating systems, each with its own view of the same team. SP + and FPI are predictive — how good is this team going forward. Elo is a running record of who beat whom. Where they disagree is usually the interesting part.

02

Against the number

What the betting market expected of this team each week, and what actually happened. The line is the crowd's forecast with money behind it, so beating it repeatedly means more than beating a bad opponent.

03

Against the country

Each bar is the distance from the FBS median in that category. Every team in the pool runs through identical code, so the comparison is like‑for‑like. These are raw rates — the panel below adjusts for who they played.

better than median worse than median blank = too few plays to rank

04

Adjusted for opponent

The same ideas as above — efficiency, explosiveness, the run game — but weighted for the quality of who was on the other sideline. A raw number against a cupcake and the same number against a playoff team are not the same number.

05

Where the ball goes

Target depth off the line of scrimmage, by direction.

06

Down & distance

What they called down by down, and how often it worked. The snap count sits in every label, because a rate off three plays is not a tendency.

07

Week by week

What this offense did against what its defense allowed, one point per game — with the conditions and the market's number alongside.

08

Who did it

Season box score by player, from the official game books. Click any column to re-sort; the tabs swap between the passing, rushing, receiving and defensive lines.

09

Head to head

Two schools, the same measurements, the same pool. Bar length is where each team sits in the national order — a full bar is the best in FBS, an empty one is last.

10

Conference by conference

Every snap taken by every member, pooled — a true league rate, not an average of team averages. Click any column to re-sort.

11

All of FBS, ranked

Every team in the country in one order. Sort by any statistic or by school name, filter to a conference, and the rank column follows whatever you sorted by. Cells are tinted toward teal as they get better and violet as they get worse.

12

How often each kind of play is called, how that compares with the rest of the country, and what it returns. Filter it by opponent or recent games, sort any column, hover a row for its definition and FBS rank, and tap a row to see every play behind it.

The slate

Every game this week with a projected margin built from SP + and a flat home‑field edge, set beside the number the market is actually offering. The gap between the two is the only column worth acting on.

The definitions

Success rate — the play gains at least half the distance to go on first down, 70% on second, all of it on third or fourth.

EPA per play — CollegeFootballData's Predicted Points Added. On defensive views it is still the opposing offense's EPA, so negative is good.

Dropbacks — pass attempts plus sacks. Sacks are charged to the passing game, not to rushing the way a box score does.

Explosive — a run of 12+ yards or a pass of 15+.

Cover margin — final margin minus what the closing line expected. Positive means they beat the number.

Line yards — the share of a run credited to the blocking rather than the back; open field yards are everything past ten.

Where it comes from

Raw play-by-play, box scores, ratings, betting lines and weather from CollegeFootballData.com — every FBS snap, both sides of the ball, re-pulled and re-parsed twice a day, at 5:00am and 4:00pm Central.

Nothing on this page is typed in by hand. If a number looks wrong, it is wrong in the play log too.
